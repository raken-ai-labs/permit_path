# Santa Monica: short-term rental rules (research as of 2026-10-16)

Method: city webpages read through a summarizing fetch tool (page-level claims are as summarized); the Municipal Code chapter was read directly on ecode360 (text of Chapter 6.20 as published there); the Administrative Regulations PDF was read directly (pdftotext). Anything the city's pages or code do not state is marked `[UNVERIFIED]`. Not a legal opinion.

## 1. Status
- **Hosted-only ("home-sharing").** Home-sharing is authorized only where the host lives on site throughout the guest's stay in the host's primary residence. Un-hosted rentals of 30 days or less are "vacation rentals" and are not authorized (SMMC 6.20.010(f),(n); 6.20.020(a); 6.20.030(a)). Source: https://ecode360.com/42735096
- Stays are capped by definition at 30 consecutive days or less (stays longer than that are outside the chapter).

## 2. Legal basis
- Santa Monica Municipal Code (SMMC) **Chapter 6.20, "Home-Sharing and Vacation Rentals"**; TOT in **Chapter 6.68**; business license in Chapter 6.04. https://ecode360.com/42735096
- Ordinance history shown in the code: Ord. 2484CCS (adopted 5/12/15, original), 2535CCS (1/24/17), 2547CCS (6/27/17), 2616CCS (9/24/19, added permit regime), **2748CCS (adopted 5/23/23)**. Same URL.
- Implementing regulations: "Home-Sharing Ordinance Rules and Regulations", revised 2019-11-21, issued under SMMC 6.20.080. https://www.santamonica.gov/media/Document%20Library/Business%20License%20Forms/INFO-Home-Share%20Admin%20Regs.pdf (saved: `ai_docs/refs/jurisdictions/santa-monica/home-share-admin-regs-2019-11-21.pdf`). These predate the 2023 ordinance; see conflicts below.

## 3. Permit or registration process and portal
- Two instruments applied for together: a **Home-Share permit** and a **Business License** (SMMC 6.20.020(a)(1); 6.20.021). Permit term equals the business license term (6.20.021(d)); not transferable (6.20.021(f)).
- Online portal: https://homesharing.santamonica.gov/ . Process page: https://www.santamonica.gov/process-explainers/how-to-apply-for-a-home-share-business-license (payment is taken after the application is processed and deemed complete; then it routes to City departments; approved applicants get a license certificate and permit).
- Expiry: valid through **June 30**; renew from **July 1** each year (same process page).
- Registry: all authorized hosts are listed on a City registry (6.20.020(b)).
- Renewal needs: declaration of prior-year compliance, accuracy affirmation, residency proof for non-owners, recertification (Admin Regs IV).
- Host must email listing URLs to the City within 10 days of approval and of any change (Admin Regs VI.B).

## 5. Primary-residence requirement and proof
- Required. Host must be an "eligible resident": an owner or long-term resident who uses the unit as primary residence (6.20.010(e)).
- "Primary residence" = usual place of return, documented by at least **two** of: motor vehicle registration, driver's license, state ID, voter registration, income tax return, property tax bill, utility bill (6.20.010(m)). One primary residence per person.
- Owner: grant deed or current property tax bill, in the name of the natural person; units held in a trust, corporation or LLC are not accepted as proof of ownership (city process page; Admin Regs III.B.2.i).
- Long-term resident (non-owner): at least 12 months of residency before applying, and a 12-month lease or written documentation of 12 more months of residence (6.20.010(j); city process page).
- Host must live on site during the stay (6.20.010(f),(i)).

## 6. Eligible and ineligible unit types
- Eligible: any "dwelling unit" (single-family, each unit of an apartment, duplex or multi-unit structure) that is the host's primary residence. Units in City-approved hotels, motels and B&Bs are outside the chapter (6.20.010(d)).
- **Only one home-share per host** (6.20.030(b)).
- **ADUs:** an ADU that received its building permit on or after **March 31, 2017** is a separate dwelling unit for this chapter (6.20.010(a)). Under the 2019 Regulations (V.A) a post-3/31/2017 ADU must itself be the host's primary residence, and a host cannot home-share both the ADU and the main house; pre-3/31/2017 ADUs may be hosted by a resident of either the main house or the ADU. Section 6.20.110 (added 2023) creates a "legalization" path for certain ADU hosts whose renewals were denied. `[UNVERIFIED: how the 2019 Regulations were revised after Ord. 2748CCS]`
- **Rent-controlled units:** not excluded. A tenant host's monthly home-share revenue may not exceed the monthly rent for the rent-controlled unit (Admin Regs V.C, citing Charter Art. XVIII, sec. 1809). The permit application asks whether the unit is rent controlled (Admin Regs III.A.9).
- Deed-restricted / affordable units: `[UNVERIFIED]` (not addressed in code chapter or Regulations as read).
- Accessory structures must contain a bedroom; storage sheds and garages are not eligible (Admin Regs VIII.K).
- Renters and HOA/CC&R restrictions: the City's authorization does not override leases, HOA bylaws or CC&Rs (Admin Regs V.B).

## 7. Night caps
- No annual night cap stated in Chapter 6.20. `[UNVERIFIED: none exists in other city documents]`
- Booking limits instead: no more than **two groups of visitors for any given date**; no more than **two listings** per platform; a host may be host for only one home-share (6.20.020(a)(6); 6.20.022(b); 6.20.030(b)).

## 8. Occupancy limits
- Total occupancy (host, other eligible residents, visitors) is the lesser of: 10 persons; one person per 200 sq ft of the dwelling unit; two persons (excluding minor children) per bedroom (6.20.020(a)(7)).
- Visitor vehicles: at most one per rented bedroom, or, in a preferential parking zone, at most two using visitor permits under Chapter 3.08 (6.20.020(a)(8)).
- "Bedroom" definition: at least 70 sq ft, door, access to a bathroom without crossing another bedroom (6.20.010(b)).

## 9. TOT (rate and registration)
- **17% for home-share room rentals** (15% for hotel/motel rooms), both effective March 1, 2023, following Measure CS (Nov 8, 2022). Code chapter 6.68 (originally Ord. 625CCS, 1963). https://www.santamonica.gov/transient-occupancy-tax
- Reports due the 1st of each month for the prior month; payment due by the last day of that month; 10% late penalty plus another 10% after 30 more days (same page).
- Host collects and remits TOT, in coordination with any platform (6.20.020(a)(3)). Registration mechanics for TOT: the page does not describe them. `[UNVERIFIED]`
- Also reported on that page: a Tourism Marketing District assessment applies to hotels by ADR band ($2.30/$6.25/$7.25 per room night for 2026); not stated to apply to home-shares. `[UNVERIFIED: applicability to home-shares]`

## 10. Insurance
- Liability insurance of at least **$500,000**, or each transaction conducted through a platform that provides equal or greater coverage (6.20.020(a)(9)). City says Airbnb and HomeAway/VRBO currently provide it; hosts listing only there need not submit proof (process page; Admin Regs III.B.3).

## 11. Platform obligations
(SMMC 6.20.050)
- Collect and remit TOT as the host's agent.
- Regularly disclose to the City each listing, responsible persons, address, length of stay and price paid (subject to law).
- **Must not complete a booking** unless the unit is on the City registry at the time it receives a fee.
- Must not take fees for services ancillary to a vacation rental or an unregistered home-share.
- Safe harbor for web-only platforms that comply; subpoena duty remains (6.20.050(e); 6.20.100(e)). Interpreted subject to federal and state law (6.20.050(f)).
- Hosts must display the business license number, on-site presence statement, occupancy, vehicle limit and two-group limit in every ad (6.20.022(a)).
- Litigation context (not a code source): the city page index lists press releases on the Ninth Circuit upholding the ordinance and a settlement with Airbnb; not read in full. `[UNVERIFIED]`

## 12. Penalties
- Infraction up to **$750**, or misdemeanor up to **$1,000** and/or up to six months in County Jail; also administrative fines under Chapters 1.09 and 1.10; cost reimbursement, back TOT and disgorgement of rental revenue; private injunctive actions with fee shifting; administrative subpoenas (6.20.100). Platforms are liable for 6.20.050 violations.
- Permit denial/revocation grounds: false statements; two or more citations in the prior year may support denial in the City's sole discretion (Admin Regs III.F, IV.A, IX.C).
