## 1. "Business" thresholds (STAT 1798.140(d)(1))
- Text: a for-profit entity that collects consumers' PI (or on whose behalf it is collected), determines purposes and means, does business in California, and meets one or more of: (A) gross revenue over $25,000,000 in the preceding calendar year as of Jan 1, "as adjusted" under 1798.199.95(d)(5); (B) alone or in combination annually buys, sells, or shares PI of 100,000 or more consumers or households; (C) derives 50 percent or more of annual revenue from selling or sharing PI.
- CPI-adjusted figure: CPPA FAQ states $26.625 million, effective Jan 1, 2025 (https://cppa.ca.gov/faq.html). The OAG page still says "over $25 million" (https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa). Next adjustment date [UNVERIFIED].
- Application: prong (A) is Raken's total revenue, not PermitPath's; Raken's revenue [UNKNOWN, not in repo]. Prong (B) counts buying/selling/sharing only, not mere collection. 1798.140(d)(2) also reaches an entity that controls or is controlled by a business and shares common branding and PI.
- If not a "business": the Act's duties (notice, rights, contracts, minimization) do not attach by their terms, but see 1798.140(d)(2) (parent/affiliate with common branding) and that Raken processes data as a vendor to Airbnb: whether Airbnb or Raken is the business, and whether Raken is Airbnb's service provider for any data, depends on who determines purposes and means. Not resolved by the text alone [UNVERIFIED how CPPA treats this].
- Penalties if covered: up to $2,500 per violation, $7,500 per intentional violation or violation involving minors, CPI-adjusted (STAT 1798.155(a), 1798.199.90(a)). Private right of action only for breaches (STAT 1798.150; OAG page: up to $750 per consumer per incident statutory damages, 30-day cure notice).

## 2. Notice at collection and privacy policy
- STAT 1798.100(a): notice at or before collection of categories of PI and sensitive PI, purposes, whether sold or shared, and retention period per category or the criteria (1798.100(a)(3)).
- REG 7012(c)-(e): notice must be where consumers encounter it, e.g. link near webform fields (7012(c)(2)); if not given at or before collection the business "shall not collect" (7012(d)). Contents (7012(e)): categories incl. sensitive PI; purposes; sold/shared per category; retention per category or criteria; opt-out link if sold/shared; privacy policy link. Must meet REG 7003(a)-(b) (plain, readable format).
- REG 7011: privacy policy. Must describe consumer rights, and, when ADMT is used for a significant decision, the opt-out and access rights (7011, sub-items on ADMT). Update at least every 12 months (1798.130(a)(5), referenced in REG 7062(g)). Full list of 7011(e) items: [UNVERIFIED: I read only the ADMT lines].
- Anonymous use still collects PI if it includes IP address, device identifiers, uploaded-document contents, or address. Whether the in-memory read of uploads is "collection" is not addressed in the text I read [UNVERIFIED]; STAT 1798.140 defines "collects" broadly (receiving by any means) [UNVERIFIED: definition not quoted here].
- Sensitive PI list (STAT 1798.140(ae)): SSN, driver's license, state ID, passport numbers; precise geolocation; account log-in or financial account numbers with credentials; and others. Uploaded permits, IDs or lease documents may contain these [depends on product]. A home address is PI; whether a rental property address is "precise geolocation" is [UNVERIFIED].

## 3. Consumer rights, deadlines, verification
- Rights: know/access (1798.110, 1798.115), delete (1798.105), correct (1798.106), opt out of sale/sharing (1798.120), limit sensitive PI use (1798.121), non-retaliation (1798.125). Opt-out of sale/sharing and Do Not Sell links apply only if Raken sells or shares PI (REG 7013; opt-out preference signals REG 7025). Raken sends Airbnb aggregate data only; whether aggregate/deidentified data is exempt depends on 1798.140(m) "deidentified" [text exists at STAT line for (m); requirements not quoted here: UNVERIFIED].
- Deadlines (REG 7021): confirm receipt within 10 business days; respond within 45 calendar days from receipt (clock runs during verification; unverifiable may be denied); one extension of up to 45 more days (90 total) with notice and reason. The same timelines apply to ADMT access and ADMT appeal requests.
- REG 7004: methods must be easy, minimal steps. Number of required request methods (REG 7020) [UNVERIFIED: not read].
- Verification of non-accountholders (REG 7062): know categories: "reasonable degree of certainty," e.g. matching 2 data points (7062(b)); know specific pieces or ADMT access: "reasonably high degree of certainty," e.g. 3 data points plus signed declaration under penalty of perjury (7062(c)); delete/correct: reasonable or reasonably high depending on sensitivity and risk (7062(d)); must deny know-specific-pieces and ADMT access if unverifiable (7062(f)). Example 2 (7062(e)(2)) addresses no-account apps: may verify by information only the app user would know or by responding to a device notification. If no reasonable verification method exists, say so in the response, explain in the privacy policy, and re-evaluate every 12 months (7062(g)). General rules: REG 7060 [UNVERIFIED: not read].
- Registered users with a password-protected account fall under REG 7061 [UNVERIFIED: not read].

## 4. Service provider / contractor contracts
- Definitions: STAT 1798.140(ag) (service provider), (j) (contractor). Contract must prohibit: selling/sharing; retaining/using/disclosing outside specified business purposes or commercial purposes; use outside the direct business relationship; combining with other PI (except as allowed) (1798.140(ag)(1)(A)-(D)). Also STAT 1798.100(d) agreement terms.
- REG 7051(a) contract must: prohibit selling/sharing; identify specific business purpose(s), not generic; limit retention, use, disclosure to those purposes; prohibit use outside direct relationship and combining; require compliance with CCPA and same level of privacy protection, with examples of assisting with consumer requests, the cybersecurity audit, risk assessment, ADMT requirements, and reasonable security (Civ. Code 1798.81.5); grant the business audit/testing rights (example: at least once every 12 months); require notice if the provider can no longer comply; grant rights to stop and remediate unauthorized use; enable the business to honor consumer requests. 7051(b): subcontractors need a compliant contract. 7051(c): failing to do due diligence or enforce can weaken the business's defense.
- Applies to Google Cloud and the model provider only if each is processing PI on Raken's behalf under a written contract meeting the above; otherwise the disclosure may be a sale/share to a "third party" and then REG 7053 and notice duties apply. I did not read either provider's current terms [UNVERIFIED: whether Google Cloud DPA or the model provider's terms contain these clauses, including no-training and no-retention terms].
- Co-host / expert referral: STAT 1798.140(ad)(2)(A) and (ah)(2)(A): a business does not sell or share PI when "a consumer uses or directs the business to intentionally disclose" PI or interact with third parties. A host-initiated referral falls in that carve-out by its text; a Raken-initiated or default referral is not covered by that text. Third-party contract terms for actual sale/share: REG 7053. "Third party" definition: STAT 1798.140(ai).

## 5. Risk assessments and ADMT (REG Articles 10 and 11)
Dates:
- Regulations effective 2026-01-01 (CPPA announcement; FAQ).
- Risk assessment required before starting a REG 7150(b) activity; legacy processing must have an assessment by 2027-12-31 (REG 7155(b)); first submissions (attestation plus summary) for assessments done in 2026-2027 due 2028-04-01 (REG 7157(a)(1)); retain for as long as processing continues or 5 years after completion, whichever is later (7155(c)); review at least every 3 years, update within 45 days of a material change (7155(a)(2)-(3)).
- ADMT requirements: compliance by 2027-01-01 for uses before that date; any use on or after it must comply when used (REG 7200(b); FAQ).

Is PermitPath's score or verdict "ADMT"?
- ADMT definition (REG 7001(e)): technology that processes PI and uses computation to replace or substantially replace human decisionmaking; "substantially replace" means output used to make a decision without human involvement; human involvement requires a reviewer who can interpret the output, review other relevant information, and has authority to change the decision (7001(e)(1)). Excluded if they do not replace human decisionmaking: calculators, databases, spreadsheets, etc. (7001(e)(3)).
- The article applies only to ADMT used to make a "significant decision" (REG 7200(a)), defined (7001(ddd)) as a decision resulting in provision or denial of: financial or lending services; housing; education enrollment or opportunities; employment or independent contracting opportunities or compensation; healthcare services. Housing (7001(ddd)(2)) means a building used as a home, residence, or sleeping place including temporary occupancy; ADMT that provides or denies housing solely on availability or payment is not a significant decision. Advertising is excluded (ddd)(6).
- What the text says on PermitPath: a readiness verdict and score are outputs of deterministic code over a rules table. They are not on their face a decision by Raken to provide or deny financial services, housing, education, employment or healthcare to the host. Whether a STR permit or registration counts as "housing" under (ddd)(2), or whether an Airbnb listing decision based on the score would be a "significant decision" by Airbnb, is not answered by the text [UNVERIFIED; no CPPA guidance found]. If Airbnb used the score to deny or allow a host's listing without human review, the risk shifts to Airbnb's own use and to whether Raken's outputs are used for that decision. This is a fact question for counsel.
- If it were in scope: Pre-use Notice (REG 7220) at or before collection, with the specific purpose (not generic), right to opt out and how, right to access ADMT, non-retaliation, how the ADMT works (categories of PI affecting output, type of output and how it is used, the alternative process if the consumer opts out) (7220(c)); trade secrets and security-sensitive information may be omitted (7220(d)). Opt-out (REG 7221): must offer unless the human-appeal exception (7221(b)(1): designated human reviewer with authority to overturn, easy appeal process) or the admission/hiring and work allocation exceptions (7221(b)(2)-(3)) apply; two or more opt-out methods (7221(c)). Access to ADMT: REG 7222 [UNVERIFIED: not read in detail]. Verification for ADMT access: reasonably high certainty (REG 7062(c)).
- Risk assessment triggers (REG 7150(b)): selling/sharing PI; processing sensitive PI; ADMT for significant decision; inference from systematic observation in educational/employment settings; inference based on presence in a sensitive location; training ADMT or facial recognition/ID technology on consumers' PI. Processing sensitive PI (for example ID numbers in uploads) would trigger a risk assessment; content of assessment is REG 7152 [UNVERIFIED: not read in detail].

## 6. Cybersecurity audits (REG Article 9, 7120-7124)
- Required if (REG 7120(b)): (1) the business met 1798.140(d)(1)(C) (50 percent of revenue from selling or sharing PI) in the preceding calendar year; or (2) it met (d)(1)(A) (revenue threshold) and processed PI of 250,000 or more consumers or households, or sensitive PI of 50,000 or more consumers, in the preceding calendar year.
- Deadlines (REG 7121): first report by 2028-04-01 if 2026 revenue over $100M; 2029-04-01 if 2027 revenue $50M-$100M; 2030-04-01 if 2028 revenue under $50M; annual thereafter by April 1. Certification to CPPA per announcement: https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2025/20250923.html.
- Auditor independence 7122; scope 7123 [UNVERIFIED: not read in detail].
- Note: the thresholds depend on counts of California consumers processed, which PermitPath may not reach in year one; Raken's revenue is needed for (2).

## 7. Retention and minimization
- STAT 1798.100(c): collection, use, retention and sharing must be reasonably necessary and proportionate to the purposes collected or another disclosed compatible purpose. STAT 1798.100(a)(3): disclose retention period per category or criteria; no retention longer than reasonably necessary for each disclosed purpose.
- REG 7002: same standard; purposes judged by reasonable consumer expectations (7002(b)) including relationship, type and amount of PI, source/method, clarity of disclosures, and how apparent third-party involvement is (7002(b)(5): a consumer may not expect disclosure to a service provider whose role is not apparent). Compatible-purpose test in 7002(c). Secondary uses (e.g. training models on user data, product analytics) are tested under this section.
- Notice at collection must state retention per category (REG 7012(e)(4)). Risk assessment records: retention under REG 7155(c). Deletion rules: STAT 1798.105; REG 7022 [UNVERIFIED: exceptions not read].
- Reading uploads "in memory" supports minimization only to the extent the document is not persisted or logged; whether the model provider retains prompts is a vendor-terms question [UNVERIFIED].
