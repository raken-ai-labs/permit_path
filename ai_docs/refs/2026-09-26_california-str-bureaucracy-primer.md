# California / Los Angeles Short-Term Rental Bureaucracy — A Primer for Non-US Readers

Written 2026-09-26. Audience: colleagues in Mexico and Chile who are smart business people but new to how US local government works. Short-term rental (STR) means a stay of fewer than 30 nights — a vacation rental, an Airbnb, a room let by the night.

## 1. Who governs what — and why an address's "city" may not be its real government

In the US, there is no single national permitting authority for rentals. Power is split in layers:

- **State** (California) sets the outer rules — building codes, environmental law, some tenant-protection law, and increasingly some STR-specific rules. A state is roughly the size and legal weight of a sovereign province — closer to a Mexican estado or a Chilean región, except a US state has its own constitution, courts, and legislature (like a mini-country).
- **County** (Los Angeles County) is a layer below the state that runs services — courts, health, some roads, tax collection — for a large area. LA County contains 88 separate incorporated cities plus large **unincorporated areas** that have no city government of their own; the county runs those areas directly.
- **City** (e.g., City of Los Angeles, Santa Monica, Pasadena) is its own government with its own council, mayor, police, and — critically for this primer — its own zoning code and its own STR ordinance. The City of Los Angeles is one of the 88 cities inside LA County, and it is enormous (about 4 million people), but it is legally just one municipality among many, not the county government.
- **Unincorporated area**: land inside the county boundary that never formed its own city government. It uses the county's rules, not a city's. Example: much of the Santa Monica Mountains, parts of East LA, some Malibu-adjacent coastline.

**Why this matters practically**: a mailing address like "Malibu, CA 90265" or "Los Angeles, CA 90291" is a US Postal Service ZIP-code label, not a legal jurisdiction. The USPS assigns a "city" name to a ZIP code for mail-routing purposes only. The actual government that regulates a property — issues permits, collects tax, enforces zoning — is determined by the parcel's legal boundary, not its postal city. A host who is told their listing is "in Malibu" by their mailing address might actually be inside the City of Los Angeles, or in unincorporated LA County, or in the separate City of Malibu — three different STR regimes. **(inferred** as a general USPS mechanism; confirmed for LA specifically by the county/city boundary maps referenced in Section 2 and 4 below.)

To find the true jurisdiction for a parcel, hosts use LA County's or the City's own GIS parcel lookup (see APN/ZIMAS below), not their mailing address.

- **APN (Assessor's Parcel Number)**: the unique ID number LA County's Assessor gives every piece of land, used to identify a specific parcel in every government system (tax, zoning, permits). Rough equivalent: the **rol de avalúo** (Chile) or a **clave catastral** (Mexico) — the tax/cadastral ID for a specific property.
- **Zoning**: the city or county rule that says what a given parcel may legally be used for (single-family home, apartment building, commercial, etc.) and under what conditions. Closest rough equivalent: **uso de suelo** designations in Mexican or Chilean municipal planning.

## 2. City of Los Angeles Home-Sharing Ordinance (HSO), end to end

The City of Los Angeles — the single largest STR jurisdiction in the region — runs its STR program under the **Home-Sharing Ordinance (HSO)**, administered by the LA City Planning Department. Source: [Home-Sharing | Los Angeles City Planning](https://planning.lacity.org/plans-policies/initiatives-policies/home-sharing) (200).

### Step 1 — Eligibility

- Only a host's **primary residence** may be registered. "Primary residence" means the unit the host actually lives in most of the year — not a second home or an investment property. [Home-Sharing | LA City Planning](https://planning.lacity.org/plans-policies/initiatives-policies/home-sharing) (200).
- **Rent Stabilization Ordinance (RSO)** units are categorically excluded. RSO is LA's rent-control law: it applies to most multi-family buildings built before October 1978 and limits how much and how often a landlord can raise rent, plus when a tenant can be evicted. No RSO unit may be registered for home-sharing. Source: (secondary, via [housing.lacity.gov / RSO Overview](https://housing.lacity.gov/residents/rso-overview) search cache) — "Home-sharing is not permitted in units covered by the Rent Stabilization Ordinance."
- **Ellis Act** units are excluded for seven years after withdrawal. The Ellis Act is a California state law letting a landlord exit the rental business entirely and evict all tenants to do so (e.g., to sell the building or convert it). Units removed from the rental market under the Ellis Act cannot be used for home-sharing for seven years, with a narrow exception if all units are demolished and rebuilt more than five years after withdrawal. (secondary, via search cache of housing.lacity.gov guidance; not independently re-fetched — mark **[UNVERIFIED against primary PDF text]**.)
- Units in legally restricted **affordable housing** are excluded. (same secondary source.)
- **ADUs (Accessory Dwelling Units)** — a secondary small housing unit on a residential lot, like a converted garage or granny flat — cannot be used for home-sharing unless the ADU itself is the host's proven primary residence and the building permit predates the relevant cutoff; new ADUs generally cannot be short-term rented. Source: (secondary, via LADBS/steadily.com search cache summarizing [dbs.lacity.gov/adu](https://dbs.lacity.gov/adu)) — treat exact cutoff date as **[UNVERIFIED]** pending direct LADBS PDF confirmation.

### Step 2 — Documents required to register

Based on the Home-Sharing registration process (Los Angeles City Planning Home-Sharing Portal), a host must show:
- Government-issued photo ID matching the property address.
- Proof of primary residence (utility bill, driver's license, voter registration, or similar showing the unit as the host's home).
- If the host is a renter, not an owner: **written landlord consent** — a letter or lease clause showing the property owner allows home-sharing. (This is a US-specific document: unlike some jurisdictions where a tenant may sublet freely, California and LA generally require the landlord's explicit written permission before subletting short-term.)
- A valid **Transient Occupancy Tax (TOT) Registration Certificate** from the LA Office of Finance (see Section 3).
- A valid **Business Tax Registration Certificate (BTRC)** from the LA Office of Finance (see Section 3).
Source for the registration flow and portal: [Home-Sharing | LA City Planning](https://planning.lacity.org/plans-policies/initiatives-policies/home-sharing) (200); TOT/BTRC prerequisites confirmed via [finance.lacity.gov/transient-occupancy-tax-requirements](https://finance.lacity.gov/transient-occupancy-tax-requirements) (secondary via search cache, page itself not independently re-fetched this pass).

### Step 3 — Registration fees (official fee schedule)

Search-cache synthesis of the current Planning Department fee schedule shows conflicting numbers across sources and dates: one page (dated Feb 23 2026) cites **$183** standard registration / **$1,030** extended; most later-2026 sources cite **$89** standard / **$850** extended-review / **$5,660** for extended applications requiring further discretionary review. **[UNVERIFIED — conflicting]**: the exact current fee could not be pinned to a single quoted line from the Planning Department's own fee-schedule PDF in this pass. Recommend the team re-confirm the live number directly on the Home-Sharing Portal before quoting a dollar figure to a client. Source snapshot: (secondary, aggregated search results referencing planning.lacity.gov fee documents, not independently fetched).

### Step 4 — The 120-night annual cap and Extended Home-Sharing

- **Standard Home-Sharing**: registered hosts may rent for up to **120 nights per calendar year**. (secondary, via search cache of planning.lacity.gov content) **[UNVERIFIED against primary PDF]**.
- **Extended Home-Sharing**: to exceed 120 nights, a host must separately qualify — generally by holding a valid registration for at least six months or having hosted at least 60 days, and by having no disqualifying nuisance violations. (secondary, via search cache) **[UNVERIFIED against primary PDF]**.

### Step 5 — Renewal

Home-sharing registration must be renewed **annually**; the registration number must appear on every listing and every advertisement. (secondary, via search cache of planning.lacity.org content — direct FAQ PDF returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch, see Sources.)

### Step 6 — Enforcement, penalties, and what a denial looks like

- The City uses its general **Administrative Citation Enforcement (ACE)** program and **Administrative Nuisance Abatement (ANA)** tools against non-compliant hosts. (secondary, via search cache.)
- If a host receives **two Verified Citations**, their Home-Sharing registration is **immediately revoked**, and they are barred from home-sharing **for one year** from whichever is later: the date of the second citation, or the conclusion of any appeal on it. (secondary, via search cache citing LAMC provisions.)
- **Denial** of an application typically stems from failing an eligibility check above — most commonly: the unit is not provably the host's primary residence, the unit is RSO-covered, or required documents (landlord consent, TOT/BTRC certificates) are missing or don't match the applicant's name/address.

### Step 7 — Appeals

Appeals of a citation or a denial are heard by the **Area Planning Commission**, under LAMC (Los Angeles Municipal Code) Section 12.22 A.32(h)(4) — a specific numbered section of the city's zoning law, the LA equivalent of a **reglamento de zonificación municipal**. If a host contests a citation and prevails entirely, any suspension is lifted. (secondary, via search cache.)

## 3. Other regulatory layers a City-of-LA host must clear

- **TOT (Transient Occupancy Tax)** — a per-stay hotel tax charged to the guest and remitted by the host to the city. In LA City, the rate is **14% of the rent charged** (including cleaning fees) for stays of 30 nights or shorter. A **TOT Registration Certificate** is required within 30 days of starting to operate, and returns are due **monthly, by the 25th of the following month** — even a "zero" month must be filed. Source: (secondary, aggregated from search of [finance.lacity.gov](https://finance.lacity.gov/transient-occupancy-tax-requirements) and [finance.lacity.gov/sharing-economy](https://finance.lacity.gov/sharing-economy); pages not independently re-fetched with a clean 200 body this pass). Rough equivalent for the team: TOT is like an **impuesto al hospedaje / ISH** that some Mexican states charge on lodging.
- **Business Tax Registration Certificate (BTRC)** — separate from TOT, this is LA's general business license: anyone earning income from a business activity in the city, including renting a room, must register for a BTRC with the Office of Finance. Rough equivalent: a **licencia de funcionamiento municipal**.
- **HOA (Homeowners' Association) and CC&Rs** — many condos and planned developments in the US are governed by a private homeowners' association, whose rules are recorded against the property title as **CC&Rs (Covenants, Conditions & Restrictions)**. An HOA can prohibit or restrict short-term rentals entirely, independent of what the city allows — this is private contract law layered on top of public law. A host must check their own HOA's CC&Rs even if the city permits home-sharing. **(inferred** as general California HOA law; not city-specific, no single URL applies.) Rough equivalent: rules under a Latin American **régimen de propiedad horizontal / condominio**.
- **Landlord consent** — as above, required in writing for any renter (non-owner) host.
- **Insurance** — standard homeowner's or renter's insurance policies typically exclude commercial short-term-rental activity; hosts are commonly expected to obtain STR-specific liability coverage (through Airbnb's included host protections or a separate policy). **(inferred** as general industry practice, not a specific LA ordinance requirement; no government URL directly mandates a specific policy for LA home-sharing in the sources reviewed.)
- **RSO / Ellis Act** — covered above; the practical effect is that a large share of LA's older apartment stock can never be legally home-shared.
- **ADU rules** — covered above.
- **Fire-zone / wildfire emergency rules (post-January 2025 fires)** — see Section 5.

## 4. Unincorporated LA County and nine other cities

### Unincorporated LA County (areas with no city government of their own)

LA County's own STR ordinance (amending Title 22, the Zoning Code, and Title 7, Business Licenses) has been **adopted and is in force** — this is not a draft. Source: [LA County Planning — Short-Term Rentals Ordinance](https://planning.lacounty.gov/long-range-planning/short-term-rentals-ordinance/) (200).
- Registration is required through the county's **Treasurer and Tax Collector (TTC)**: contact the STR Unit for a six-digit account number and activation code, create an account at lacountystr.munirevs.com, then submit the application and pay the registration fee. Source: [ttc.lacounty.gov/str](https://ttc.lacounty.gov/str/) (200, per earlier fetch).
- STRs are limited to **registered primary residences**; unhosted stays are capped at **90 nights per calendar year**; ADUs, junior ADUs, guesthouses and pool houses may **not** be used as STRs. Source: [LA County Planning](https://planning.lacounty.gov/long-range-planning/short-term-rentals-ordinance/) (200).
- The county's TOT rate for unincorporated areas is **12%** (secondary, per TTC FAQ PDF search cache — not independently re-fetched with clean text this pass).
- **Coastal-zone properties in unincorporated county are currently exempt** from this registration scheme, because the county's Local Coastal Program amendment allowing STR regulation there has not yet been certified by the California Coastal Commission (see Section 5). Source: [ttc.lacounty.gov/str](https://ttc.lacounty.gov/str/) (200).

### Nine other cities (one line each)

1. **Santa Monica** — Permitted, hosted-only ("home-sharing"); un-hosted "vacation rentals" remain banned; a business license is required. [How to Apply for a Home-Share Business License](https://www.santamonica.gov/process-explainers/how-to-apply-for-a-home-share-business-license) (200).
2. **West Hollywood** — Permitted, but only for owner-occupied units where the host is present the entire stay; fines run 400–800% of the advertised nightly rate for violations. (secondary: [weho.org home-sharing license page](https://www.weho.org/city-government/city-departments/community-safety/neighborhood-and-business-safety/business-licensing-and-permits/home-sharing-license), HTTP 403 to direct fetch.)
3. **Pasadena** — Permitted with a Short-Term Rental Permit; host must occupy the property at least nine months of the year; pure "vacation rentals" (no residency) are banned citywide; TOT 12.11% plus a tourism-district assessment of 3.89–4.89%. [City of Pasadena — Short-Term Rental Regulations](https://www.cityofpasadena.net/planning/short-term-rental-regulations/) (200).
4. **Long Beach** — Permitted; non-primary-residence STRs are capped at **800 citywide**, one primary + one non-primary registration per operator; unlimited unhosted nights for a registered non-primary unit. [City of Long Beach — STRs](https://www.longbeach.gov/lbcd/enforcement/strs/) (200).
5. **Glendale** — Permitted under a "Home-Sharing" ordinance requiring a license; specific hosted/unhosted distinction not independently confirmed. (secondary: [glendaleca.gov home-sharing page](https://www.glendaleca.gov/government/departments/community-development/neighborhood-services/licensing/home-sharing), HTTP 403 to direct fetch) **[UNVERIFIED]**.
6. **Malibu** — Permitted under a Short-Term Rental Permit (Ordinance No. 468), but the city's stricter hosted-only/primary-residence ordinance (No. 472) **cannot take effect until the California Coastal Commission certifies the city's Local Coastal Program amendment** — still pending as of today. [Malibu — Short-Term Rental Program](https://www.malibucity.org/820/Short-Term-Rental-Program) (200).
7. **Beverly Hills** — Banned citywide in all single-family and multi-family units (BHMC 10-3-508); minimum lease term of 12 months; fines up to $5,000/day for violations. [Beverly Hills — Short-Term Rentals](https://www.beverlyhills.org/278/Short-Term-Rentals) (200).
8. **Culver City** — Currently prohibited (activity is technically illegal); enforcement is paused while the city drafts a new regulatory program expected later in 2026. [Culver City — Short Term Rentals](https://www.culvercity.gov/shorttermrentals) (200, redirected from culvercity.org).
9. **Burbank** — Prohibited by omission — short-term rentals are simply not a listed permitted use under the zoning code (BMC 10-1-602); no active licensing path exists. (secondary: [burbankca.gov fact sheet](https://www.burbankca.gov/burbank-fact-sheet), HTTP 403 to direct fetch.)

## 5. State and coastal law that matters

- **SB 346 — the "Short-Term Rental Facilitator Act of 2025."** Confirmed as the correct, real bill (the brief's reference to "SB 346" is accurate). Authored by Senator Durazo; **chaptered (signed into law) October 13, 2025**. It lets a local government require booking platforms like Airbnb to report the physical address (with 9-digit ZIP code), and, if needed, the assessor's parcel number, listing URL, or ADU identifier, of every STR during a reporting period — quarterly, or monthly if the city requires monthly tax filings. Non-compliant platforms can face administrative fines. Crucially, this law is **not automatic**: a city or county must adopt its own ordinance invoking it before it applies locally. Source: [leginfo.legislature.ca.gov, SB-346](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB346) (200).
- **SB 1318 (2025–2026 session, still pending)** — "Coastal resources: local coastal program: coastal development permit: non-owner-occupied short-term rentals." Would let the Coastal Commission approve rules restricting non-owner-occupied STRs in the coastal zone on local housing-need findings. Still moving through the Senate as of this writing (last amended in 2026) — **not yet law**. Source: [leginfo.legislature.ca.gov, SB-1318](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB1318) (200).
- **California Coastal Act of 1976** (Public Resources Code §30000 et seq.) — the state law protecting the California coastline. Any regulation of land use — including STR bans or caps — within the mapped "coastal zone" is treated as "development" and generally requires either inclusion in a **certified Local Coastal Program (LCP)** or a **Coastal Development Permit (CDP)**. A city cannot simply ban STRs in its own coastal strip without the state Coastal Commission's sign-off; courts have blocked at least one city (Manhattan Beach) from enforcing an uncertified coastal STR ban. This is the same rule stalling Malibu's stricter ordinance and exempting unincorporated coastal-zone parcels from LA County's registration system (Sections 2 and 4). Source: [leginfo.legislature.ca.gov, PRC §30000](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PRC&sectionNum=30000) (200); Coastal Commission guidance memo (secondary: [documents.coastal.ca.gov STR memo](https://documents.coastal.ca.gov/assets/la/Short_Term_Vacation_Rental_to_Coastal_Planning_&_Devt_Directors_120616.pdf), 200 but PDF text not cleanly extractable this pass — treat CDP/LCP requirement as corroborated via secondary search summary, not a verbatim primary quote).
- **Coastal Commission (California Coastal Commission)** — the state agency, not a city or county body, with authority over the coastal zone described above. Rough equivalent for the team: a national/state-level environmental-coastal authority that can override local municipal land-use decisions in a protected strip of coastline — there isn't a precise Mexican/Chilean equivalent, but it functions like a superior planning authority that pre-empts local zoning in a defined geographic band.

## 6. What Airbnb already does for LA hosts

- **Registration/permit number field**: Airbnb requires hosts in regulated jurisdictions to add their government registration/permit number under Listings → Policies and rules → Laws and regulations → Regulations; "the law requires all Hosts to display their registration number on their listing." Source: [Airbnb Help — Registration numbers](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/871) (200 — this specific article is written for San Francisco but the mechanism is the same one applied to LA-regulated listings).
- **Airbnb City Portal**: Airbnb's public tool for local governments, giving cities data/insights and enforcement-support resources. "A first-of-its-kind solution for cities partnering with Airbnb... Use industry-first tools and resources to help develop and enforce fair, balanced short-term rental policies." Source: [airbnb.com/cityportal](https://airbnb.com/cityportal) (200).
- **Tax collection agreement**: Airbnb automatically collects and remits the City of LA's **14% Transient Occupancy Tax**, plus a **$3.30-per-night administrative fee**, on qualifying reservations of 30 nights or fewer booked and paid through Airbnb. Source: [Airbnb Help — Occupancy tax collection](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2297) (200). However, whether the general "areas with automatic tax collection" list breaks LA out explicitly, or only lists "California" broadly, was not confirmed in this pass. - **Hosts remain independently responsible for compliance** even when Airbnb collects/remits TOT automatically — a host must still hold a valid TOT Registration Certificate and file a monthly TOT return (even a "zero" return) with LA's Office of Finance. (secondary: search-cache summary of finance.lacity.gov and third-party compliance guides; not independently re-fetched with a clean primary quote this pass.)

## 7. Where hosts get stuck (mapped to Section 2's steps)

- **Step 1 (Eligibility)**: Hosts most often fail here because they cannot prove primary residence (utility bills in someone else's name, a second home used occasionally) or because their unit is RSO-covered without the host realizing it — RSO status is not obvious from a lease; it must be checked against the building's construction date and unit history, e.g. via the city's ZIMAS parcel-lookup tool.
- **Step 2 (Documents)**: Renters frequently stall because they never obtained written landlord consent, or their TOT/BTRC certificates are still pending when they try to submit the Home-Sharing application (the certificates are prerequisites, so sequencing matters — TOT/BTRC first, HSO registration second).
- **Step 3 (Fees)**: Because the current fee schedule could not be pinned to one authoritative number in this research pass (see the $89 vs. $183 discrepancy above), a prospect quoting an exact dollar figure to a client risks being wrong; this is a concrete gap to close before pricing conversations.
- **Step 4 (120-night cap / Extended Home-Sharing)**: Hosts commonly discover the cap only after being cited, because nothing in the booking flow stops them from accepting a 121st night; Extended Home-Sharing eligibility (6 months' registration or 60 hosted nights, no nuisance violations) is a second gate many hosts don't know exists until they're already over the limit.
- **Step 5 (Renewal)**: A lapsed annual renewal silently turns a previously legal listing into a violation — hosts who set up once and never returned to the portal are a recurring enforcement target.
- **Step 6 (Enforcement/penalties)**: The "two citations = one-year ban" rule is a hard cliff-edge with no gradual escalation — a host who successfully appeals citation #1 but loses on a nearly identical citation #2 can be locked out for a full year.
- **Step 7 (Appeals)**: Appeals go to the Area Planning Commission, not a court and not the Planning Department that issued the citation — a body many hosts don't know exists or how to petition.
- **Layered jurisdictions (Sections 1, 4)**: A property whose postal address says one city but whose legal jurisdiction is unincorporated county, or vice versa, is a recurring source of hosts applying to, or paying tax to, the wrong government entirely.
- **Coastal zone (Section 5)**: Hosts in or near Malibu, coastal Long Beach, or unincorporated coastal county face a moving target — local rules are frequently stalled or exempted pending California Coastal Commission certification, so "what the city website says" and "what's actually enforceable today" can diverge.
- **Emergency-rule confusion (Section 5)**: The overlapping, time-limited fire-related relief (county STR relief, two gubernatorial executive orders, statewide price-gouging caps, several individual cities' urgency ordinances) had disputed expiration dates even across official-adjacent sources in this research pass — a live risk of a host relying on an already-expired relief measure.

## Verified 2026-09-27 (read the official PDFs directly; each returned 200)
- **No appeal of a denial: CONFIRMED.** Admin Guidelines p13: "There are no appeals of a closed, denied, or withdrawn application. Applicants may re-apply at any time." (Issued June 28, 2019.) Appeals exist only for Extended Home-Sharing decisions (15 days, Area Planning Commission) and for disputes about a citation. https://planning.lacity.gov/ordinances/docs/HomeSharing/adopted/AdminGuidelines.pdf
- **Business tax certificate: hosts DO get one.** The portal registers the host for a BTRC with the TOT classification, and the BTRC number matches the Home-Sharing number. The "3 or fewer units exempt" rule is for long-term rentals and doesn't apply here. Same PDF, section III.1.
- **Fee: $192** (application or renewal), Extended $1,030, Extended discretionary review $15,166, administrative hearing $20,980. Source: Zoning Code Article 15 fee schedule v2025.1.0, Sec. 15.5.1, p15-40. https://planning.lacity.gov/odocument/43f716cf-71fa-4cfc-9788-6f17c6e6c0fa/Art15-Fees_v2025.1.0._Adopted.pdf. The $89 / $850 / $5,660 figures come from the 2019 FAQ and Admin Guidelines and are out of date. Caveat: Article 15 belongs to the new Chapter 1A zoning code. Confirm it applies citywide (inferred yes, since fees apply to Sec. 5C.3.2 Home-Sharing).
- **Fines, effective Sept 1, 2024** (CPI-adjusted each year; a Sept 2025 update may exist): platform $1,211.33/day; advertising an ineligible unit $605.73/day; going over the 120-night cap $2,422.89/day. https://planning.lacity.gov/odocument/e8fa00a0-58e3-4c97-a7de-b22be9846e4a/
- **Renewal:** yearly; the renewal notice goes out 30 days before expiry; the host must submit their Home-Sharing records. FAQ (June 26, 2019).
- **Pending number:** a complete application gets a Pending Registration Number and the host can list right away. If it's later rejected, all listings come down immediately.
